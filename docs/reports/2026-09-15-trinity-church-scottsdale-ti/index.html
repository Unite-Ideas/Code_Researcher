F-01 · Health / Food Service

Cafe hand sink cannot become a sanitizer station

Significant Verified primary source

“Hand wash sink at cafe was ruled cost-prohibitive — replace with hand sanitizer station.”

Applicable code
FDA Food Code, 2017 Ed. §2-301.16(A)(3), §5-203.11 — adopted by Maricopa County Environmental Health Code Ch. VIII §2
maricopa.gov — Ch. VIII §2, Food Establishments · county-hosted mirror — FDA Food Code 2017 (verbatim text confirmed here)
The rule, applied here

A dedicated hand sink is required at any bar preparing TCS drinks — espresso and steamed milk qualify. Sanitizer is only ever a supplement used after washing at a sink, never a substitute. Maricopa's plan-review guide requires a hand sink accessible from “all bars” for an Open Food Service and Beverage Establishment, which this cafe is.

Problem

High probability of rejection at Maricopa County Environmental Services plan review — not a gray area under the adopted code.

Proposed solutions
  • Compact plumbed ADA hand sink (9×9″–15″) at the end of the counter run — smallest footprint, fully compliant.
  • Point-of-use tankless electric water heater dedicated to that sink, avoiding a long hot-water run from the building heater.
  • Reconfigure the counter layout to free 12–18″ for the sink — often cheaper than moving walls.
  • Portable hand-wash cart: recognized by the county mainly for catering/mobile use; not recommended as the permanent fix.
Verify: confirm the establishment classification with Maricopa County Environmental Services Plan Review, (602) 506-6824, before finalizing the cafe layout.
F-02 · Process / Permit Strategy

“Field revision” plan is a real compliance risk

Significant Verified primary source

Plan: get the permit issued on the original drawings, then implement egress door, security door, playland, restroom, and baptistry changes afterward as field revisions — avoiding a full city re-review.

Applicable code
2021 IBC, Ch. 1, §107.4, Amended Construction Documents
ICC — 2021 IBC §107.4
The rule, applied here

“Work shall be installed in accordance with the approved construction documents, and any changes made during construction that are not in compliance with the approved construction documents shall be resubmitted for approval as an amended set of construction documents.” The Building Official's judgment on scope governs, since the code gives no numeric threshold. Egress/security door changes, the playland occupancy reclassification, new restroom fixtures, and new baptistry plumbing are all ordinarily plan-reviewed items.

Problem

If caught in the field: stop-work order, a required retroactive amended-document submittal, paid re-inspection of completed work, and a delayed certificate of occupancy for the B-to-A conversion.

Proposed solutions
  • Disclose the site-visit changes to the plan reviewer now and get their determination in writing.
  • Selectively resubmit the highest-risk items: egress door location/hardware, the security door, and the lobby/playland reconfiguration.
  • Route the restroom stall increase and baptistry plumbing/water heaters through at least a plumbing plan revision.
  • Request a pre-application meeting with Scottsdale Building Safety given the compounded occupancy change.
Verify: confirm directly with Scottsdale Building Safety / One Stop Shop, (480) 312-2500, before proceeding on the current strategy.
F-03 · Zoning / Parking

Off-site parking strategy needs a recorded agreement, not a handshake

Significant Likely, secondary source

“When we told the city we're going to break the tenant improvement plans into separate packages for separate permits, they said we need to prove ample parking for each piece… they've already got over 200 committed to the building just south of them.”

Applicable code
Scottsdale Revised Code, Appendix B, Art. IX Table 9.103.A (parking ratio) · §9.107 (Remote Parking) · §9.104.E/F (Shared Parking / Parking Master Plan)
Municode — Scottsdale zoning, Article IX Parking & Loading
The rule, applied here

Table 9.103.A requires 1 space per 4 fixed seats in the sanctuary — at 1,462 seats that is 366 spaces at code minimum, well under the design team's internal “1 per 2 seats” planning rule. The zoning code does not treat the East and West permit packages as separate sites for parking purposes unless a Parking Master Plan is filed. Off-site or shared spaces only count toward the requirement through §9.107 or §9.104.E/F: a written agreement approved by both the Zoning Administrator and City Attorney, recorded against both properties, with a 5-year minimum term, and the remote lot within 600 feet via a safe pedestrian route.

Problem

The strategy itself (TIA plus shared parking plus the garage) is a normal, code-recognized path — the risk is procedural. A recorded, City Attorney-approved agreement is required before permit issuance for any off-site spaces counted toward the requirement; an in-progress conversation with the neighbor is not sufficient. Pursuing East/West as informally separate packages without a filed Parking Master Plan risks the city requiring each side to independently prove adequacy.

Proposed solutions
  • Confirm the shared-parking neighbor's lot is within the 600-foot §9.107 distance limit via a safe pedestrian route, before relying on it in the parking calculation.
  • Get the Remote/Shared Parking Agreement drafted, approved by the Zoning Administrator and City Attorney, and recorded with the County Recorder well ahead of the West-side permit submission.
  • File a formal Parking Master Plan covering both permit packages as phases of one plan.
Verify: confirm exact current text of Table 9.103.A and §9.107 with Scottsdale Planning & Development Services; the recorded agreement itself needs sign-off from the City Attorney's Office and recording at the Maricopa County Recorder.
F-04 · Building / Structural

AVL rigging needs sealed calculations, not just attachment details

Significant Likely, secondary source

“Jerry flagged that Scottsdale jurisdictions may require structural calcs and more complete documentation (not just details), similar to other recent projects; Amplio confirmed this is doable and offered to share a sample.”

Applicable code
2021 IBC §1613.1 (ASCE 7 Ch. 13, nonstructural components) · Table 1604.5 (Risk Category III at 300+ occupants) · Ch. 17 §1705.13 (special inspections) · §107.3.4/.4.1 (deferred submittals)
ICC — 2021 IBC Chapter 16, Structural Design
The rule, applied here

Rigging, LED walls, speaker clusters, and truss attached to the roof/ceiling structure are “nonstructural components” that must be designed to resist seismic effects under ASCE 7 Chapter 13, not just detailed for gravity/attachment hardware. The 1,462-occupant auditorium is Risk Category III (over 300 people in one area), which raises the required Component Importance Factor and can trigger special inspection of the anchorage. A generic beam-clamp cut sheet does not satisfy this — it takes engineered calculations sealed by a licensed engineer, submitted as a deferred submittal under §107.3.4/.4.1.

Problem

Real risk of rejection or correction cycles at plan review if the AVL contractor's sample package is only attachment details without sealed calculations. This is the base 2021 IBC as written, not a Scottsdale-specific overlay — plan for it as a standard requirement.

Proposed solutions
  • Have the AVL contractor's engineer prepare a proper delegated-design package: sealed calculations addressing gravity and seismic loads (correct Component Importance Factor for Risk Category III) at the actual as-installed weights and attachment points.
  • Have the engineer of record formally list the rigging/AVL scope as a deferred submittal, and route the delegated engineer's calculations through the EOR for general-conformance review before submitting to the building official.
  • Confirm with the EOR whether the anchorage requires special inspection under Chapter 17 given the project's Seismic Design Category, and schedule it alongside the rest of the special-inspection program.
Verify: confirm the project's actual Seismic Design Category and resulting Component Importance Factor with the structural engineer of record, and the special-inspection trigger with Scottsdale Building Safety.
F-05 · Fire / Life Safety

Kids ministry lockdown door needs AHJ sign-off

Moderate Likely, secondary source

Plan retains both a dedicated egress-only door and a separate lockable security door on the kids ministry hallway, which now has fewer secure doors facing it.

Applicable code
2021 IBC §1010.2.8, Locking Arrangements in Educational Occupancies
iDigHardware — classroom door lock requirements
The rule, applied here

A door may lock against intruders if unlockable from outside with a key, interior egress hardware stays fully compliant, and panic/fire-door hardware is untouched. The two-door approach (egress-only door separate from the lockdown door) is a sound, common strategy that avoids stacking locks on one leaf.

Problem

Three items need the fire marshal's or plan reviewer's sign-off, not just design judgment: the space's final occupancy classification, whether the reduced secure-door count still meets exit-access and common-path-of-travel rules for the child occupant load, and that the security door stays secondary to the egress door.

Proposed solutions
  • Confirm the kids ministry space's occupancy classification with the plan reviewer.
  • Verify exit access doorway count and common path of travel against IBC Table 1006.2.1 using the actual child occupant load.
Verify: needs Scottsdale Fire Prevention / Building plan review sign-off on the specific room layout and occupant load.
F-06 · Accessibility

ADA path-of-travel: budget per phase, not by assessed value

Moderate Verified primary source

“ADA compliance required throughout: ambulatory stalls, swing-out doors, 5-foot turning radius.”

Applicable code
28 CFR §36.403, Alterations: Path of Travel · 2010 ADA Standards §202.4 · AAC R10-3-401–412
ecfr.gov — 28 CFR 36.403
The rule, applied here

Commonly misread as “20% of the building's assessed value” — it isn't. The cap is 20% of the cost of the specific alteration to the primary function area; required path-of-travel work is prioritized (entrance, then route, then a restroom, then fountains/phones) once the cap is hit.

Problem

At this scale — a multi-phase occupancy change touching large portions of the building — the obligation very likely applies broadly and repeatedly, phase by phase. A budgeting issue, not a blocker, if planned for early.

Proposed solutions
  • Document existing accessible-route deficiencies now as a baseline.
  • Budget a per-phase contingency of up to 20% of that phase's construction cost.
F-07 · Plumbing

Toddler “Jack and Jill” restrooms: lavatory-in-classroom is a genuine judgment call

Moderate Likely, secondary source

“Toddler toilet only in Jack and Jill restrooms. Separate toddler handwashing sink in classroom.”

Applicable code
2021 IPC §405.3.2, Public Lavatories · A.R.S. §36-884(2), religious-nursery child-care licensing exemption
ICC — 2021 IPC §405.3.2 · azleg.gov — A.R.S. §36-884
The rule, applied here

§405.3.2 requires the lavatory in the same room as the water closet, but only for a “public” or “employee” toilet room — a “private” single-fixture room with restricted access is not covered. An official Indiana interpretation of this identical section approved a single-user children's toilet room with the lavatory in the one classroom it served. Trinity's rooms are each shared between two classrooms (true Jack-and-Jill), a materially different fact pattern the code doesn't clearly resolve. Separately, Arizona's religious-nursery exemption (A.R.S. §36-884(2)) confirms no state child-care plumbing rules layer on top of the base IPC for this weekend program.

Problem

Genuinely ambiguous, leaning toward real compliance risk given the two-classroom-shared factor. With roughly 15 of these restrooms across the kids ministry area, a rejected assumption at plan review means costly, repetitive rework.

Proposed solutions
  • Get a written interpretation from Scottsdale Building Safety before proceeding, citing the Indiana precedent as support for treating each restroom as “private.”
  • Add one compact wall-hung lavatory inside each toilet room in addition to the classroom sink — resolves the ambiguity outright at modest added cost across the ~15 rooms.
Verify: get the “public vs. private” classification in writing from Scottsdale Building Safety plan review before relying on it across all 15 rooms. The Arizona licensing exemption holds only as long as the program stays tied to weekend religious services.
F-08 · Fire / Life Safety

Confirmed occupant loads trigger sprinkler, alarm, and voice-alarm systems

Moderate Likely, secondary source

“Auditorium Seating: 1,462 [capacity], 13,382 SF… Next Gen Auditorium: 260 people… Adults Classroom [also functions as a chapel]: 435 person capacity.”

Applicable code
2021 IBC §903.2.1.3 (sprinkler, Group A-3) · §907.2.1 (manual alarm) · §907.5.2.2 (voice/alarm communication)
ICC — 2021 IBC Chapter 9, Fire Protection Systems
The rule, applied here

Sprinklers are required throughout a Group A-3 fire area over 12,000 SF or with occupant load 300+; the main auditorium clears the 300-occupant threshold by nearly 5x, and its 13,382 SF footprint independently exceeds 12,000 SF. Manual fire alarm is required at occupant load 300+; full emergency voice/alarm communication (not just pull stations) at 1,000+. At 1,462, the main auditorium clears both. This is not a borderline call.

Problem

Not a design defect — a standard, unavoidable consequence of the Business-to-Assembly conversion at this scale, now confirmed with real numbers. The Adult Discipleship room (435, used as an overflow chapel) independently clears the same 300-occupant sprinkler/alarm thresholds and should not be treated as a lower-tier space.

Proposed solutions
  • Confirm the sprinkler and fire alarm/voice-communication scope already reflects all three assembly spaces (1,462 / 435 / 260) at their correct individual occupant loads with the fire protection engineer.
F-09 · Fire / Life Safety

Access-controlled doors: confirm hardware door by door

Minor Likely, secondary source

“Approximately 90 doors total (77 interior) to receive power and data for key card / key fob access control readers.”

Applicable code
2021 IBC §1010.2.10, Access-Controlled Egress Doors
iDigHardware — access-controlled egress doors
The rule, applied here

Where a reader controls the egress side, code requires an approach sensor that unlocks the door, fail-safe unlock on power loss, and a marked manual push-button override. This applies only where the reader affects egress-side hardware — entry-only readers with free-egress interior levers are ordinary hardware.

Proposed solution
  • Have the hardware consultant confirm, door by door, which locking topology each of the ~90 doors actually uses before installation.
F-10 · Fire / Life Safety

Roll-up doors: fine for freight, never the egress door itself

Minor Verified primary source

“Roll-up doors vs double doors may not be optimal operationally… remove roll-up at chair/table storage end if it conflicts with operations… keep roll-up only where it truly supports flexibility without creating egress issues.”

Applicable code
2021 IBC §1010.1.2, Egress Door Types
ICC — 2021 IBC §1010.1.2
The rule, applied here

Egress doors must be side-hinged swinging, pivoted, or balanced — overhead, coiling, and roll-up doors appear nowhere in the permitted types or the nine narrow exceptions. A roll-up can still be installed for freight/cart movement as long as it is never the door relied on for egress-door-type, egress-width, or exit-access requirements; a separate properly sized swinging door in the same area covers the egress function. This is exactly the direction the design team is already leaning.

Problem

Not a problem if executed as already planned: roll-up for freight only, with a separate dedicated swinging door for egress. Worth confirming a couple of secondary triggers on that swinging door rather than leaving them open.

Proposed solutions
  • Confirm whether the receiving area's occupant load reaches 50 (triggering swing-direction-of-travel) or serves a Group A fire area requiring panic hardware, and specify the swinging door's hardware accordingly.
  • If the receiving-area wall is a rated fire/smoke separation, confirm the swinging door gets a listed fire-rated assembly and any roll-up penetrating the same wall is itself a listed fire/smoke-rated rolling door assembly per NFPA 80.
F-11 · Building

Restroom door swing: likely not an issue

No problem found Likely, secondary source

“Confirm door swing direction per IBC 50-person occupancy threshold.”

Applicable code
2021 IBC §1010.1.2.1, Direction of Swing
iDigHardware — door swing & occupant load
The rule, applied here

Applies to a door serving a room or area with an occupant load of 50 or more. A 15-stall women's restroom and similarly expanded men's restroom each have a room occupant load well under 50 — the requirement clearly targets the main assembly space's own exit doors instead.

Verify: confirm this room-vs-space reading with the Scottsdale plans examiner in writing, since AHJ interpretation of “area served” can vary.

Codes that may matter later

2021 IEBC Ch. 10, §§1011.4–1011.6 Change of Occupancy — the code that actually requires the egress/occupant-load recalculation, height-and-area check, and fire-protection threshold check triggered by the Business-to-Assembly conversion. This is the umbrella citation; several of its consequences are already broken out above as their own findings.
IPC Table 403.1 Plumbing fixture counts for places of worship — verify the restroom stall counts already being planned across all three assembly spaces (1,462 + 435 + 260 occupants) against this table with a licensed code source.
Baptistry cross-connection / backflow prevention Not yet researched — localized water heaters, cold water fill lines, and trench drains at three baptistry locations may trigger backflow prevention requirements to check before rough-in.
IBC Table 1006.2.1 Exit access travel distance / common path of travel for the Next Gen (260) and Adult Discipleship (435) spaces — now that occupant loads are confirmed, each space's own exit count and travel-distance compliance should be checked individually, not just the main auditorium's.
