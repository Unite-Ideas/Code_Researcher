## Findings, most consequential first

### 1. Cafe hand wash sink cannot be replaced with a hand sanitizer station -- SIGNIFICANT

**The question:** Can the specialty coffee cafe's hand wash sink, ruled cost-prohibitive, be replaced with a hand sanitizer station?

**The code:** Maricopa County Environmental Health Code Ch. VIII Sec. 2, adopting the FDA Food Code 2017 Edition (Sections 2-301.12, 2-301.14, 2-301.16(A)(3), 5-203.11, 5-204.11, 5-205.11).
Source: https://www.maricopa.gov/DocumentCenter/View/5477/Chapter-8-Section-2---Food-Establishments-PDF and https://teamhcso.com/getattachment/f70a54e6-43e7-4983-abe2-843a578bbe2d/attachment-3-applicable-fda-food-code.pdf

**The rule:** A dedicated hand sink is required at any bar preparing TCS food or drinks (espresso, steamed milk qualifies). Hand sanitizer is only ever a supplement applied after washing at a sink -- never a substitute. Maricopa County's plan-review guide explicitly requires a hand sink "centrally located, visible, and directly accessible... including all bars" for an Open Food Service and Beverage Establishment, which this cafe is.

**The problem:** High probability of rejection at Maricopa County Environmental Services plan review. This is not a gray area under the adopted code.

**Proposed solutions:**
- Compact plumbed ADA hand sink (9x9 to 15 inch wall-mount or corner unit) at the end of the counter run -- smallest footprint, fully compliant.
- Point-of-use tankless electric water heater dedicated to that sink, avoiding a long hot water run from the building's main heater.
- Reconfigure the counter layout to free 12-18 inches for the sink, often cheaper than moving walls.
- A self-contained portable hand-wash cart is recognized by the county mainly for catering/mobile use and is not recommended as a permanent, final solution.

**Verify:** Call Maricopa County Environmental Services Plan Review (602-506-6824) to confirm the establishment classification in writing before finalizing the cafe layout.

### 2. Permit strategy: implementing scope changes as field revisions carries real risk -- SIGNIFICANT

**The question:** Can the site-visit changes (playland reconfiguration, egress/security door changes, restroom stall increase, baptistry plumbing, MEP changes) go in as field revisions after permit issuance, without resubmitting to the City of Scottsdale?

**The code:** 2021 IBC, Chapter 1, Section 107.4, Amended Construction Documents.
Source: https://codes.iccsafe.org/s/IBC2021P1/chapter-1-scope-and-administration/IBC2021P1-Ch01-Sec107.4 (Scottsdale does not locally amend this section, so the base code text applies as-is; directly browser-verified against ICC's own page)

**The rule:** "Work shall be installed in accordance with the approved construction documents, and any changes made during construction that are not in compliance with the approved construction documents shall be resubmitted for approval as an amended set of construction documents." The Building Official's judgment on the scope of the change governs, since the code gives no numeric threshold. Egress door changes, security/lockdown door hardware, converting classroom space to lobby/assembly space, new restroom fixtures, and new baptistry plumbing and water heaters are all plan-reviewed items under standard code administration.

**The problem:** This is a real process risk, not routine field-revision practice. If caught, likely outcomes include a stop-work order, a required retroactive amended-document submittal, re-inspection of completed work at the contractor's cost and delay, and a delayed certificate of occupancy.

**Proposed solutions:**
- Proactively disclose the site-visit changes to the assigned plan reviewer now and get their determination in writing, even while pursuing permit issuance on the original drawings.
- Selectively resubmit only the highest-risk items -- egress door location/hardware, the security door, and the lobby/playland reconfiguration -- as a formal plan revision.
- Route the restroom stall increase and baptistry plumbing/water heater additions through at least a plumbing plan revision.
- Request a pre-application meeting with Scottsdale Building Safety given the compounded occupancy change.

**Verify:** Confirm directly with Scottsdale Building Safety / One Stop Shop (480-312-2500) before proceeding on the current field-revision strategy.

### 3. Shared/off-site parking needs a recorded agreement before permit issuance, not just an agreement in progress -- SIGNIFICANT

**The question:** Is the project's parking strategy (a Traffic Impact Analysis, shared-parking discussions with neighbors, plus the adjacent garage) sufficient to satisfy Scottsdale's zoning requirement for each separately permitted phase?

**The code:** Scottsdale Revised Code, Appendix B (Zoning Ordinance), Article IX -- Table 9.103.A (parking ratio for churches), Sec. 9.107 (Remote parking), Sec. 9.104.E/F (Shared Parking Program / Parking Master Plan).
Source: https://library.municode.com/az/scottsdale/codes/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=VOLII_APXBBAZOOR_ARTIXPALORE

**The rule:** Table 9.103.A requires 1 space per 4 fixed seats in the sanctuary, plus 1 space per 300 SF of classroom/meeting area. At 1,462 seats, code minimum for the sanctuary alone is 366 spaces -- the design team's internal "1 space per 2 seats" planning figure (~731 spaces) is roughly double the actual minimum, so that internal rule is conservative, not a shortfall. Nothing in the ordinance treats the East and West permit packages as separate legal sites -- each permit is checked against the cumulative site requirement unless a Parking Master Plan (Sec. 9.104.F) is filed to demonstrate adequacy per phase. Off-site/shared spaces only count toward the requirement through Sec. 9.107 (Remote parking) or Sec. 9.104.E/F: a written agreement approved by both the Zoning Administrator and the City Attorney, **recorded** at the Maricopa County Recorder's Office, minimum 5-year term, with the remote lot within 600 feet of the served property via a safe pedestrian route.

**The problem:** The overall strategy is a normal, code-recognized path -- the ordinance has purpose-built tools for exactly this. The real risk is procedural: a recorded, City Attorney-approved agreement is required *before* permit issuance for any off-site spaces counted toward the requirement. "In progress" conversations or a letter of intent are not sufficient at that point, and if the East/West permits are pursued as informally separate without a filed Parking Master Plan, the city could require each to independently prove adequacy.

**Proposed solutions:**
- Confirm the shared-parking neighbor's lot is within the 600-foot Sec. 9.107 distance limit via a safe pedestrian route, now, before finalizing the TIA.
- Get the Remote Parking (or Shared Parking / Parking Master Plan) agreement drafted, approved by the Zoning Administrator and City Attorney, and recorded well ahead of the West-side permit submission -- this involves another property owner and two city approvals, so it needs lead time.
- File a formal Parking Master Plan (Sec. 9.104.F) covering both permit packages as phases of one plan, so the East/West split isn't read as two independent parking obligations.

**Verify:** Confirm the exact current text of Table 9.103.A, Sec. 9.107, and Sec. 9.104.E/F against a certified Municode copy or directly with Scottsdale Planning & Development Services -- Municode's own site could not be automated-fetch-verified in this pass.

### 4. Suspended AVL rigging needs sealed structural calculations, not just attachment details -- SIGNIFICANT

**The question:** Is providing only attachment/connection details (not full structural calculations) for the suspended AVL rigging, LED walls, and speaker clusters over the main auditorium sufficient for Scottsdale plan review?

**The code:** 2021 IBC Sec. 1613.1 (nonstructural components -> ASCE 7 Chapter 13); Table 1604.5 (Risk Category III at occupant load 300+); Chapter 17, Sec. 1705.13 (special inspections); Sec. 107.3.4/107.3.4.1 (deferred submittals).
Source: https://codes.iccsafe.org/content/IBC2021P1/chapter-16-structural-design (Scottsdale's own amendment ordinance was read directly and confirmed not to touch Sec. 1613 or Chapter 17 at all -- this is the base IBC being enforced as written, not a Scottsdale-specific add-on)

**The rule:** Rigging, LED walls, and speaker clusters permanently attached to the roof structure are "nonstructural components" requiring an ASCE 7 Chapter 13 seismic (and gravity) design, not just attachment hardware detailing. At 1,462 occupants, the auditorium is Risk Category III, which raises the required Component Importance Factor and can trigger manufacturer certification or special inspection of the anchorage under IBC Chapter 17. A generic beam-clamp catalog cut sheet does not satisfy this -- it requires calculations sealed by a licensed engineer, submitted as a deferred submittal per Sec. 107.3.4.1.

**The problem:** Real risk of rejection or correction cycles at plan review if the AVL contractor's "sample package" is only attachment details without sealed calculations. Worth planning for as a standard requirement rather than unusual local strictness.

**Proposed solutions:**
- Have the AVL contractor's engineer prepare sealed calculations addressing gravity and seismic (ASCE 7 Ch. 13, correct Component Importance Factor for Risk Category III) loads for the actual as-installed truss, LED wall, and speaker cluster weights at their specific attachment points.
- Have the engineer of record formally list the rigging/AVL scope as a deferred submittal on the approved drawings per 107.3.4.1, with the delegated engineer's calculations routed through the EOR for general-conformance review before submitting to the building official.
- Confirm with the structural EOR whether the anchorage requires special inspection under Chapter 17 given the project's Seismic Design Category, and schedule it alongside the rest of the special-inspection program.

**Verify:** Confirm the project's actual Seismic Design Category and Component Importance Factor with the structural engineer of record, and the exact special-inspection trigger with Scottsdale Building Safety.

### 5. Kids ministry lockdown door plus dedicated egress door -- MODERATE, needs AHJ sign-off

**The question:** Is it compliant to keep both a dedicated egress-only door and a separate lockable security door on the kids ministry hallway, given the reduced number of secure doors?

**The code:** 2021 IBC Section 1010.2.8, Locking Arrangements in Educational Occupancies.
Source: https://idighardware.com/2023/03/decoded-current-requirements-of-the-i-codes-for-classroom-door-locks/

**The rule:** A door may be locked to keep intruders out, provided it is unlockable from outside with a key or approved means, the interior egress hardware remains fully code-compliant, and panic hardware/fire door hardware/closers are not modified. The two-door approach (a hardware-clean egress-only door separate from the lockdown security door) is a sound, commonly used strategy that avoids stacking locks on one leaf.

**The problem:** Three items are not resolved by document research alone: the final occupancy classification of the kids ministry space (which determines which locking section applies), whether the reduced secure-door count still meets required exit access doorway counts and common path of travel for the child occupant load, and confirmation that the security door stays secondary to the dedicated egress door.

**Proposed solutions:**
- Confirm the kids ministry space's occupancy classification with the plan reviewer.
- Have the architect verify exit access doorway count and common path of travel against IBC Table 1006.2.1 using the actual child occupant load.

**Verify:** Needs Scottsdale Fire Prevention/Building plan review sign-off on the specific room layout and occupant load -- not resolvable by research alone.

### 6. Accessible path-of-travel spend: budget for it now, per phase -- MODERATE

**The question:** How much of the budget may be required for accessible path-of-travel upgrades, and is it based on alteration cost or building assessed value?

**The code:** 28 CFR 36.403 (federal ADA/DOJ), 2010 ADA Standards Section 202.4, Arizona Administrative Code R10-3-401 through 412.
Source: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-28/chapter-I/part-36/subpart-D/section-36.403 and https://regulations.justia.com/states/arizona/title-10/chapter-3/article-4/r10-3-401/

**The rule:** This is commonly misunderstood as a percentage of the building's assessed value -- it is not. The cap is 20% of the cost of the specific alteration to the primary function area, and the required path-of-travel work is prioritized (accessible entrance, then route, then a restroom, then phones/fountains) if the cap is reached.

**The problem:** For a project of this scale and scope, the obligation very likely applies broadly and repeatedly, phase by phase. This is a budgeting/scoping issue, not a blocker, but needs planning rather than late discovery.

**Proposed solutions:**
- Document existing accessible-route deficiencies now as a baseline.
- Budget a per-phase contingency up to 20% of that phase's construction cost.

### 7. Jack-and-Jill toddler restrooms: toilet-only room plus classroom sink is a genuine gray area -- MODERATE

**The question:** Is it code-compliant for each toddler "Jack and Jill" restroom (shared between two classrooms) to contain only a toilet, with the required handwashing lavatory instead located in the adjoining classroom?

**The code:** 2021 IPC Section 405.3.2, Public lavatories; Arizona child-care licensing exemption, A.R.S. Sec. 36-884(2).
Source: https://codes.iccsafe.org/s/IPC2021P1/chapter-4-fixtures-faucets-and-fixture-fittings/IPC2021P1-Ch04-Sec405.3.2 and https://www.azleg.gov/ars/36/00884.htm

**The rule:** IPC 405.3.2 requires the lavatory in the same room as the water closet, but only for "public" or "employee" toilet rooms -- a "private" room (a single fixture, restricted access) is not covered. An official Indiana interpretation of this same section approved a single-classroom-access children's toilet room with the lavatory in the classroom, reasoning that it wasn't "public." Trinity's rooms are shared between *two* classrooms, a closer call the code doesn't clearly resolve. Separately, Arizona exempts a church's weekend nursery/kids-ministry program from state child-care licensing (A.R.S. 36-884(2), no stated hours cap), so Arizona's stricter childcare-facility plumbing rules don't apply on top of the base IPC here.

**The problem:** Genuinely ambiguous, leaning toward a real risk given the two-classroom-sharing factor. With roughly 15 of these restrooms across the kids ministry area, a rejected assumption at plan review means costly, repetitive rework.

**Proposed solutions:**
- Get a written interpretation from Scottsdale Building Safety before proceeding, citing the Indiana precedent as support for treating each restroom as "private."
- The safer fix: add one compact wall-hung lavatory inside each toilet room in addition to the classroom sink -- resolves the ambiguity outright at modest added cost across the roughly 15 rooms.

**Verify:** The Arizona child-care exemption is verified and solid as long as the program stays weekend/service-based. The IPC "public vs. private" call for a two-classroom-shared restroom is a genuine plan-reviewer judgment -- get it in writing before relying on it across all 15 rooms.

### 8. Occupant loads now confirmed: sprinkler and voice-alarm requirements apply, not just to the main auditorium -- MODERATE

**The question:** Now that the space program gives real seating numbers, do the sprinkler and fire alarm/voice-communication requirements apply, and to which spaces?

**The code:** 2021 IBC Sec. 903.2.1.3 (sprinklers, Group A-3); Sec. 907.2.1 (manual fire alarm); Sec. 907.5.2.2 (emergency voice/alarm communication).
Source: https://codes.iccsafe.org/content/IBC2021P1/chapter-9-fire-protection-systems

**The rule:** Sprinklers are required in a Group A-3 fire area over 12,000 SF, or occupant load 300+, or not at the level of exit discharge. Manual fire alarm is required at occupant load 300+; a full emergency voice/alarm communication system at 1,000+. The main auditorium (1,462 seats, 13,382 SF) clears every one of these thresholds by a wide margin. The Adult Discipleship room, also used as an overflow chapel, independently seats 435 -- also past the 300-occupant threshold on its own. The Next Gen auditorium seats 260, under the 300 mark on its own.

**The problem:** Not a design defect -- these are unavoidable consequences of the occupancy conversion at this scale, now confirmed with real numbers instead of an estimate. The Adult Discipleship/chapel room's 435-person capacity independently triggering the same thresholds as the main auditorium is worth double-checking isn't being treated as a lower-tier space in the fire protection scope.

**Proposed solutions:**
- Confirm the sprinkler and fire alarm/voice-communication scope already reflects all three assembly spaces at their correct individual occupant loads with the fire protection engineer.

**Verify:** These are well-established, unamended IBC thresholds; confirm exact current wording against a licensed source before it goes into a stamped fire-protection narrative.

### 9. Access-controlled doors: confirm hardware topology door by door -- MINOR

**The question:** Do the roughly 90 doors receiving key card/fob readers need special egress hardware?

**The code:** 2021 IBC Section 1010.2.10, Access-Controlled Egress Doors.
Source: https://idighardware.com/2022/08/2021-ibc-commentary-on-egress-doors-with-access-control/

**The rule:** Where a reader controls the egress-side hardware of a required exit door, the code requires an approach sensor that unlocks the door, fail-safe unlocking on power loss, and a marked manual push-button override. This only applies where the reader affects the egress side -- if the interior lever/panic hardware always operates freely regardless of the reader, ordinary hardware rules apply instead.

**The problem:** Not a blocker, but a verification item across ~90 doors, best resolved before installation.

**Proposed solution:** Have the electrical engineer/hardware consultant confirm, door by door, which locking topology is used.

### 10. Roll-up doors on the receiving-area egress path: the design team's own instinct is the compliant answer -- MINOR

**The question:** Can a roll-up/overhead freight door at Production/Receiving serve as, or sit along, a required means of egress path?

**The code:** 2021 IBC Section 1010.1.2, Egress door types.
Source: https://codes.iccsafe.org/s/IBC2021P1/chapter-10-means-of-egress/IBC2021P1-Ch10-Sec1010.1.2 (directly browser-verified against ICC's own page)

**The rule:** Egress doors must be side-hinged swinging, pivoted, or balanced door types, with nine narrow exceptions (small private rooms, detention areas, critical-care rooms, single-dwelling-unit doors, revolving doors, certain power-operated sliding assemblies, and small manually-operated sliding doors). Overhead/coiling/roll-up doors appear nowhere in the permitted list. A roll-up door can still be used for freight, as long as a separate, properly sized swinging door handles the egress function in the same area -- exactly what the design team is already leaning toward ("keep roll-up only where it truly supports flexibility without creating egress issues").

**The problem:** Not a problem if executed as already planned. Two secondary items are worth confirming rather than leaving open.

**Proposed solutions:**
- Confirm whether the receiving area's occupant load reaches 50 (triggering swing-direction-of-travel) or requires panic hardware, and specify the swinging door's hardware accordingly.
- If the receiving-area wall is a rated fire/smoke separation, confirm the swinging door gets a listed fire-rated assembly independent of its egress compliance, and any roll-up door in the same rated wall is itself a listed fire/smoke-rated rolling assembly per NFPA 80.

### 11. Restroom door swing direction -- likely not an issue

**The question:** Do the expanding restroom doors need to swing in the direction of egress travel under the IBC 50-person threshold?

**The code:** 2021 IBC Section 1010.1.2.1, Direction of swing.
Source: https://idighardware.com/2012/10/door-swing-and-encroachment/

**The rule:** This applies where a door serves a room or area with an occupant load of 50 or more. A 15-stall women's restroom and similarly expanded men's restroom each have a room occupant load well under 50, so on the plain text of this section the restroom doors themselves are likely not required to swing in the direction of travel -- that requirement clearly applies to the main assembly space's own exit doors instead.

**Verify:** Confirm this room-vs-space interpretation with the Scottsdale plans examiner in writing, since AHJ interpretation of "area served" can vary.
