---
topic: ADA path-of-travel alteration threshold ("the 20 percent rule")
last_verified: 2026-09-15
---

A common point of confusion: this is NOT "20% of the building's assessed value." It is a
federal DOJ/ADA Title III regulation:

- **28 CFR § 36.403, "Alterations: Path of travel"** — https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-28/chapter-I/part-36/subpart-D/section-36.403
- **2010 ADA Standards for Accessible Design, § 202.4** — cross-references back to
  36.403(f) for the disproportionality test. https://www.ada.gov/law-and-regs/design-standards/2010-stds/

**The 20% figure is 20% of the cost of the alteration to the primary function area** — the
renovation/construction cost of the specific altered work — not 20% of the whole building's
value or replacement cost. Once accessible path-of-travel work (the route from the site
arrival point through the altered area, plus the restrooms/drinking fountains/phones
serving it) would cost more than 20% of that alteration's cost, the additional cost is
"disproportionate" and not required, but the 20% must still be spent, prioritized per DOJ
guidance: accessible entrance first, then route to the altered area, then at least one
accessible restroom, then telephones/fountains.

**Arizona layer**: the Arizonans with Disabilities Act (A.R.S. § 41-1492 et seq.),
implemented at Arizona Administrative Code Title 10, Ch. 3, Art. 4 (R10-3-401 through
R10-3-412), expressly incorporates the DOJ's 2010 ADA Standards and 28 CFR 36.401-36.406 by
reference. No Arizona-specific numeric variant beyond the federal 20% figure was found as of
last verification.

For a multi-phase project touching large portions of an existing building (e.g. a
tenant-improvement occupancy change), each discrete alteration/phase triggers its own
20%-of-that-phase's-cost analysis for the route serving it. Recommend documenting existing
accessible-route deficiencies as a baseline before construction and budgeting a per-phase
contingency up to the 20% cap.
